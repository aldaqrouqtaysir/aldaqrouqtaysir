# Taysir Al Daqrouq

I’m a Computer Science student at United Arab Emirates University and an American raised in the UAE. I’m interested in applied machine learning, statistical computing, and research software—especially work that can turn messy environmental or public-service data into conclusions people can trust.

I enjoy working at the boundary between research and engineering: defining a question carefully, building a reproducible system, comparing it against strong baselines, and being honest about limitations and negative results. I am still early in my undergraduate journey, so an important part of my current work is developing the mathematical, algorithmic, and independent programming foundations needed to contribute to deeper research.

## What I’m working toward

My long-term goal is to become a research engineer who can contribute to rigorous machine-learning research while also building software that is useful, understandable, and reproducible.

I hope to pursue mentored research, technically substantive internships, publication-quality work, and eventually graduate study in machine learning or a related field. I am particularly interested in opportunities where I can take real technical ownership, receive serious feedback, and produce work that can be evaluated honestly.

My main interests include:

- Reliable machine learning under distribution shift
- Data-efficient learning and careful model evaluation
- Probability, statistics, and experimental design
- Environmental and public-sector applications of data
- Reproducible research software
- Full-stack engineering as a way to turn technical ideas into usable tools
- Trustworthy AI systems with clear limitations and human oversight

## How I approach work

- Evidence before hype
- Strong simple baselines before unnecessary complexity
- Reproducible code, automated tests, and documented decisions
- Clear claim boundaries and honest limitations
- Negative results as useful evidence rather than failures to hide
- Building systems I can explain and defend independently
- Revising my conclusions when the evidence changes
- Balancing ambition with depth, responsibility, and continued learning

## Beyond the technical work

Growing up and studying in the UAE as an American has shaped how I think about collaboration and public-interest problems. It has also made me attentive to how people from different backgrounds can understand the same issue differently.

I value curiosity, careful listening, collaborative leadership, and creating environments where people can contribute meaningfully. I want the work I produce to be technically credible, but I also want it to be understandable and useful to people beyond the person who wrote the code.

## Selected work

- **[Dubai PM₂.₅ Cross-Site Forecasting](https://github.com/aldaqrouqtaysir/dubai-pm25-cross-site-forecasting)** — A first-author applied machine-learning study evaluating leave-one-site-out forecasting across four Dubai community-sensor streams, with leakage-controlled validation, strong baselines, uncertainty analysis, and reproducible evidence.

- **[Inferential Safety Lab](https://github.com/aldaqrouqtaysir/inferential-safety-lab)** — Research software using known-answer simulations to compare statistical-analysis pipelines under controlled missingness and measurement-error scenarios, with deterministic replay and explicit failure reporting.

- **[SAIS Academic Navigator](https://github.com/aldaqrouqtaysir/academic-pathfinder-app)** — A deterministic academic-planning application that evaluates up to 1,728 schedules from a 57-course catalog and supports student and counselor workflows.
